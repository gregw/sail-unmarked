Design note · settled model

The course lifecycle

Course, variant, snapshot — and how you know what you are editing.

A club edits its courses on the morning of a race, while boats are already looking at them. This note fixes the three things a course can be, the two kinds of geometry it can be made of, and the rule that tells an editor whether the change in front of them lands on one course or on twenty. It is the model only. Where the data lives, and how a boat registers for a start, come after.

Status

The vocabulary and the rules below are settled and are what the editor is being built to. Registration, start broadcast, divisions on the boat side and the storage layout are explicitly not settled and are not described here.

The crossing model — lines, ends, sense and extent — is in the project brief and is unaffected by any of this.

01The problem this solves

Two facts about this system pull against each other.

The first is that courses are edited live. The wind backs twenty degrees at nine in the morning and somebody drags the windward line before the fleet leaves the dock. That is a feature, and the brief calls it out as one.

The second is that a record names geometry, not a name. What a boat sailed is identified by a content hash over resolved positions and crossing order, because a course called manly‑to‑shark on Tuesday and the same name on Thursday can be two different pieces of water. Comparing times across an edit would be comparing different races.

Put together, they mean that editing is a publishing act with consequences, and the editor needs to say what the consequences are before the change lands. Three questions have to have answers:

  • When I move this line, whose racing did I just change?
  • Which of my courses are no longer what I last published?
  • How do I keep a well‑known course for Div 1 and a shortened one for Div 2 without maintaining two unrelated copies of the same idea?

Every rule below exists to answer one of those.

02The three nouns

Course what a club publishes

The unit a club advertises and a boat joins. Saturday Pointscore, or Manly to Shark. A course has a name, notes, and one or more variants. It has no geometry of its own.

Variant what an editor edits

An editable course design belonging to exactly one course. Div 1, Div 2, short course — or simply the variant, where a course has only one and nobody ever thinks about the word.

A variant is a sequence of steps over the club's points and lines. It is mutable, always, right up to the warning gun. Nothing a boat has sailed depends on it staying still.

Snapshot what a boat sails

An immutable, fully inlined capture of one variant at one moment. Every point and line is resolved to coordinates at the instant of capture; nothing in a snapshot resolves anything by name afterwards.

A snapshot is identified by its revision — the twelve‑hex‑character content hash the records already use — and named by the tool as course/variant/datetime. It is therefore a named, dated pointer to a revision.

Why a variant belongs to exactly one course

Because that is what makes the dangerous question answerable. Whose racing did I just change? has an answer only if the edge from geometry to racing runs one way through a structure with no shortcuts. A variant shared between two courses would be a course by another name, and the shared thing would need its own name, its own publication and its own dirty state — which is to say it would need to be a course.

Shape that is genuinely common between two courses is shared at the level below, as a named line, which is exactly what named lines are for.

03Named and ad‑hoc geometry

A point or a line is either named or ad‑hoc.

  Named Ad-hoc
Has an id Yes — sow-and-pigs No. It exists only inside the variant that uses it
Lives in The club's points and lines list The step that references it
Edited from The Points or Lines tab, and from a course with consent The Courses tab, freely
Moving it May affect other variants and other courses Affects exactly one variant, by construction
Naming is identity, not sharing

A named point is not named because it is shared. It is named because it is a thing people refer to — Sow and Pigs is Sow and Pigs whether one course uses it or nine. Sharing is a consequence of several courses choosing to refer to the same thing, and it is measured by counting references, never declared by a flag.

This is why the earlier shared / local naming was dropped. It stated as policy something that is really an observation, and it would have gone stale the moment a second course adopted a "local" mark.

A line's two ends are independent

Moving one end of a line does not detach the other. One end may be a surveyed feature the whole club knows and the other a handle dropped this morning for this course; forcing them to share a fate would either promote the handle to a name nobody wants or detach the feature everybody relies on. The unit of the named/ad‑hoc decision is the point, and the line follows whatever its ends happen to be.

04The tab is the scope of the edit

This is the rule that answers am I editing one course or many? — and it is answered before the edit begins, by where you are standing, rather than by a modal question after the fact.

Tab You are editing Blast radius
Points, Lines The club's named geometry Everything that refers to it. The edit reports what it dirtied
Courses One variant of one course That variant — unless you consent to more
The invariant

From the Courses tab you can never change the geometry of a course you are not in, and you can never do it by accident. Widening the scope beyond the current variant always takes an explicit answer to an explicit question.

Editing geometry from within a course

Drag a point while a variant is open and the editor looks at what else uses it. There are four cases and they are the whole of the interaction:

What else uses it What happens
Nothing — it is ad‑hoc It moves. No question, no notice.
Only this variant It moves. Named, but nobody else is holding it.
Sibling variants of this course Ask, offering three: move it for all of them; make it ad‑hoc here and move the copy; or promote a new named point, shared by these variants, and move that.
Another course Ask, offering make it ad‑hoc here, or — where this course has more than one variant — promote a new named point shared by all of them. Moving it for the other course is never on offer; that is the invariant.

Promote is what replaces a scoping mechanism for now. A point wanted by Div 1 and Div 2 but not by the rest of the club is a real thing that deserves a name, and the tool supplies one — manly-to-shark/windward — from the course it was promoted in. It is offered only where it would differ from the other two: with a single variant it is exactly "make it ad‑hoc", and where only siblings are involved it is exactly "move it for all".

Detaching a point does not detach the line

Making a point ad‑hoc copies every line that named it, with only that end repointed at the copy. The other end still names the club's point, because a line's two ends are independent and one of them may be the surveyed feature that made the line worth having.

The tool names things, not the user

Scoped names are generated. Asking people to follow a naming convention produces a namespace held together by discipline, which is to say for about a season. If a full scoping mechanism is ever needed, generated names in this shape are what it will be built from.

Editing named geometry from its own tab

Here the whole club is in scope, deliberately — this is where you correct a survey or move a mark that has genuinely moved. The edit proceeds, and then reports: this dirties Div 1 and Div 2 of Saturday Pointscore, and the Twilight short course.

Nothing is blocked and nothing is cascaded. The consequences are made visible and then tracked, which is what section 6 is about.

05Snapshots inline everything, immediately

When a snapshot is taken, every named reference in it is resolved to coordinates and written in. The snapshot keeps no names.

Lazy inlining — keeping the names and resolving them later, so that a change to a named point could be seen to affect published courses — is attractive and was seriously considered. It is rejected, for two reasons that reinforce each other:

  • The revision is a hash over resolved coordinates. A snapshot that resolves names later has a hash that can silently stop describing its own contents. The one identifier that records depend on would become unreliable, which defeats its only purpose.
  • The living variant already answers the impact question. A snapshot is taken from a variant, and that variant stays behind, still holding its named references. So "which published courses use Sow and Pigs?" is answered by looking at variants, where the answer is current, rather than at snapshots, where it would be a question about the past.
The resolution

Snapshots are always inlined on creation. The ad‑hoc variant they were created from remains, so we still know which courses a change to a named point affects.

Immutability and impact tracking turn out not to be in tension; they are just jobs for two different objects.

It follows that editing named geometry never modifies a snapshot, and therefore never modifies anything a boat has already been handed. A published course changes only when somebody publishes a new snapshot. That is the safety property the whole model is arranged around.

06State is derived, never stored

Resolve a variant, hash it, and compare that with the revision of its latest snapshot. Everything an editor needs to know follows from that one comparison:

State Means What to do
unpublished No snapshot has ever been taken Nothing, until it is wanted
current The hash matches the latest snapshot Nothing. What is published is what is designed
dirty The hash differs — edited since the last snapshot, here or through a named point Review the difference, then snapshot and publish
incomplete It will not resolve — a point with no coordinates, a step with no line Fix it. It cannot be snapshotted

No dirty flag is written anywhere, and none can go stale, be missed by an edit path, or survive an undo it should not have. It is a fact about the data, computed when asked.

Dirty is visible at both levels. The course list marks a course dirty if any of its variants is; selecting the course shows which. That is the race‑morning workflow: scan for orange, open it, see that moving the windward line dirtied Div 1 and Div 2 but not the Twilight course, check both, publish both.

Review means a diff

"Dirty" on its own only says something changed. What an editor needs before publishing is what changed: this line moved 340 m, this step was added, the course is 0.4 nm shorter. The comparison between the last snapshot and the current resolved variant is the natural place to get it, since both sides are fully resolved geometry.

07Snapshot and publish are two steps

Taking a snapshot captures a design. Publishing chooses which snapshot boats get. Keeping them apart lets a course be prepared on Thursday and released on Saturday, and lets a bad release be undone by pointing back at the previous snapshot rather than by re‑editing anything.

Publishing a variant replaces that variant's previous publication. It is a pointer move, not an append — a course has one live design per variant, and offering a fleet a choice of vintages would be a way to start a race with two different courses on the water.

A boat is handed what was published

Joining serves the published snapshot, never the live variant. A course with nothing published cannot be joined at all. So a mark dragged on race morning changes nothing for anybody on the water until somebody publishes again — which is the safety property the whole model exists for, stated as an endpoint.

Two scopes, one operation

  • One variant. Div 2's course is shortened; nothing else changes. Publish Div 2.
  • Several variants, atomically. A line common to Div 1, Div 2 and Div 3 is moved on race morning. All three must change together or the divisions are racing to inconsistent instructions. Publish all three, or none.

These are the same operation at different scope: move a set of publication pointers, all or nothing. The atomicity is the constraint with teeth — it means the pointers being moved have to be committed together, which will shape where publications are stored. Since a shared line can dirty variants across different courses, that scope is probably club‑wide rather than per‑course.

08Templates

A club has shapes it uses repeatedly — a windward/leeward, a triangle‑sausage‑sausage — which are the starting point for a day's racing rather than a course anybody sails. Two windward/leeward fleets on the same day need two independently positioned courses, so a single perpetually‑edited design cannot serve.

A template is a variant marked as such. It is edited like any other variant. The one thing it cannot do is be snapshotted — and from that, everything else follows:

# a template can never be snapshotted
#   ⇒ it can never be published
#   ⇒ no boat can ever join it
#   ⇒ it is permanently unpublished, never dirty

No separate rule keeps templates out of the join path, and none keeps them out of the race‑morning dirty list. One restriction produces all of it.

Cloning

To race a template you clone it. The clone is an ordinary variant, with the template flag cleared — otherwise the copy could not be snapshotted either.

  • A variant may be cloned into any course, existing or new. That is what makes two windward/leeward fleets work: clone the template into Saturday A and again into Saturday B, position each for its own fleet, publish each separately. It does not weaken "a variant belongs to one course" — cloning creates a new variant that belongs to the target.
  • A whole course may be cloned, which is just cloning all of its variants. This matters because a good template usually carries divisional structure already — Div 1 sails three laps, Div 2 sails two — and cloning variant by variant would lose the grouping the template existed to capture.
  • A clone shares named geometry, and copies ad‑hoc geometry. A template built on named lines produces clones that all move when a named line moves — usually what is wanted at a club with surveyed marks, and the reason the next point matters.
Translate and rotate are a bulk detach

Sliding or turning a freshly cloned course to suit today's wind converts every named line it inherited into ad‑hoc geometry, in one gesture. That is correct — you are moving this course, not the club's marks — but it is the one action that quietly makes most of a course ad‑hoc, so it should say this will detach 6 shared lines before it does it.

The warning on editing a template

Worth showing once, and worth being reassurance rather than caution, because people assume the opposite of what is true: editing a template does not affect courses already cloned from it. Those clones are independent. The edit changes only what future clones start from.

09Deletion

Snapshots outlive everything that produced them.

  • Deleting a variant or a course does not delete its snapshots. They stay exactly as they were, retrievable by revision. They simply leave the dirty conversation, because nothing living is being compared against them any more.
  • A snapshot that has been replaced by a newer publication is likewise kept.
  • Snapshots go only when explicitly deleted.

The reason is the record. A record names a revision, and a record whose geometry cannot be retrieved is a time with no course attached — unscoreable, unprotestable, and not comparable with anything. A course being retired is not a reason to make last season's results meaningless.

10A worked sequence

# design
create course        Saturday Pointscore
create variant       Div 1              — unpublished
edit                 place lines, order steps
snapshot                                    — Saturday Pointscore/Div 1/2027-06-06T08:14
publish              Div 1                  — boats can now join

# the second division is a variation on the first, not a new idea
clone variant        Div 1 → Div 2
edit                 drop a lap
snapshot
publish              Div 2

# race morning: the wind backs
Lines tab            drag windward 340 m
                     → dirties Div 1, Div 2 of Saturday Pointscore
                     → dirties Twilight/short course
review               diff each against its last snapshot
snapshot             Div 1, Div 2
publish              Div 1 + Div 2 atomically
                     Twilight is left dirty on purpose — it races Wednesday

11What this note does not settle

  • Storage. Where courses, variants, snapshots and publications live on disk, and in what format. The atomic multi‑variant publish is the constraint that will decide most of it.
  • Registration and start. How a boat registers, how a division is determined or asked for, how a start timer reaches a registered fleet, and how a live edit is broadcast to boats that already hold a snapshot.
  • Force‑registration and the club's view of who is out there.
  • Scoped names as a mechanism. Generated names such as manly-to-shark/windward are a convention today, not a namespace. Deliberately on the back burner.
  • Write protection. A named point that published courses depend on can still be dragged by anybody with the page open. Which is a specific instance of the general gap: there is no authentication on any write.
